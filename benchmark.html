What the metric columns mean
Score
Composite ranking score = 0.39·Reach + 0.33·Language + 0.28·Consensus (weights in config.HEURISTIC_WEIGHTS). Liveness is excluded from the score (weight 0.00 — stored as metadata only). The reach term is log1p-saturated so a pageview leader cannot crush a consensus-backed brand.
Reach
Audience breadth proxy: log-normalized Wikipedia pageviews (English + up to 2 local editions), strongest edition wins, scaled by in-country language audience share. Measured over the full pool; structurally-present outlets without measurable pageviews (broadcasters, agencies, radio) keep a presence floor (REACH_PRESENT_FLOOR) instead of zero. 1.0 = most-viewed outlet in the country.
Lang
Language match: 1.0 when the outlet publishes in an official (or de-facto majority) language of the country, otherwise 0.5. A neutral floor — not a multilingualism penalty.
Consensus
Discovery-source agreement: the share of active discovery sources (Wikidata, Wikipedia lists, ABYZ, OnlineNewspapers) that surfaced the outlet. 1.0 = found by every active source.
Liveness
Metadata only — not part of the score. Live HTTP probe of the outlet's homepage: 0.5 reachable, +0.25 HTTPS, +0.25 recent (< 7-day) publish timestamp. Bot-challenged sites (HTTP 403) score 0.75 stored — a challenge proves the site is live and HTTPS-served, but blocked sites can't show recency. A failed probe is retried once. Only the top 7 candidates per country are probed; everyone else carries the neutral default 0.5 (an unprobed 0.5 is not a failed probe). Excluded from scoring because big brands that block bots / can't expose timestamps would be falsely bumped down.
